Made deliberately

Every decision was made for a two-year-old.

Toddlers can't read, can't take being rushed, and shouldn't be sold to. So the app has none of it.

No advertising

Not a banner, not a video, not one. Nothing in the app links out to anywhere else.

No text at all

Nothing to read, so nothing to translate — a child anywhere in the world plays the same game.

No timers, no score

Nothing counts down and nothing keeps score. A child can sit on one screen for as long as they like.

No way to lose

A wrong tap is answered gently and never punished. There is no red X anywhere in the app.

Purchases behind a parental gate

The single optional purchase sits behind a gate a toddler can't pass. Payment is handled entirely by the store.

No data collected

No accounts, no analytics, no tracking. Settings stay on the device and leave with the app.

One purchase, all games

Half the games are free. A single purchase unlocks the rest, in all four worlds, on every device signed in to the same store account.

Four worlds

Each world teaches something the others don't.

Sixteen games in all. No two ask the child to do the same thing.

Where a child starts: familiar animals, one clear action per game, and a friendly voice for every creature.

  • Matching food to the animal that eats it
  • Recognising shapes through shadows
  • Caring for something — washing a muddy pig
  • Object permanence, by finding who is hiding

A step up: the same gentle rules, but now the child has to remember, to listen, and to get the timing right.

  • Memory, by finding pairs
  • Recognising animals by their real recorded call
  • Timing, swinging a monkey from vine to vine
  • Big and small, giving every animal a bush its own size

The liveliest world — everything in it moves, and everything reacts to being touched.

  • Tracking a moving target and choosing the right one
  • Fine motor control, guiding a fish through the reef
  • Colour, by sorting fish into matching coral
  • Free play with sound — a whole underwater orchestra

The newest world. Nothing in it is scary: the T-rex is silly, the volcano only smokes, and every dinosaur is glad to see you.

  • Patience and reward, hatching an egg tap by tap
  • Planning a path, laying stones across the lava
  • Part and whole, digging up bones and putting a skeleton together
  • Balance, stacking a tower so a dinosaur can reach the leaves
